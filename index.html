The ASN Validator
⌄

So I wrote a desktop tool that reads every segment in plain English, fixes what it can safely fix, explains what it cannot, and writes the report that goes back to the supplier. It runs as a one-click executable on the team's machines.

Plain English, not EDI

Every segment of every pack sequence, spelled out — "S = simplified pack," not PAC+1+S. Nothing hidden: a missing segment shows up as a red row.

Corrections you can trust

Auto-fixes pack counts, splits CPS loops, recounts trailers. The despatched quantity is never touched, invented serials are flagged, and a fix that makes things worse rolls itself back.

Watches the folder

Drop an ASN in and it's validated within seconds, logged to CSV, and sorted into _passed / _failed — no babysitting.

Supplier-ready reports

One click builds an executive summary, error frequencies, and corrective actions — as emailable text and polished HTML, split per supplier when a batch spans several.

Teaching a camera to audit boxes
⌄

A Python and OpenCV pipeline reads packaging barcodes in batches and cross-checks them against the packaging instruction and the shipping notice, while a vision model I trained myself classifies box types on the receiving line. I taught it to say "unclassified" rather than guess, which is the part that makes it worth trusting. Audit effort moves off sampling and onto the exceptions that actually matter.

I trained the camera's classification model myself — showing it every box family, tuning confidence thresholds, and teaching it to say "unclassified" instead of guessing.
